# Known limitations

These boundaries are part of the current `0.1.x` release decision. They must not be hidden by a broader maturity label.

## Release and support boundary

- No stable Synex `1.0` release exists yet. One documented `synex_core` tree passed its frozen [Production-Beta gate](release-readiness.md) on 2026-08-25. That is a production-oriented beta inside its immutable supported profile, not acceptance of later Core changes or a framework-wide stable release.
- The beta target covers one frozen `synex_core` tree only. `synex_groups`, `synex_accounts`, `synex_entities` and `synex_world` are separate Experimental Alpha domains. Accounts has 59 server-only contracts and 17 migrations, but its real MariaDB, FXServer, restart/crash-recovery, restored-upgrade, exact-candidate review, and owner maturity/publication decision remain open. Entities has 33 server-only versioned definitions across 32 names and four migrations, but its real MariaDB, FXServer/OneSync, restart/recovery, cluster and exact-candidate decision remain open. World has seven server-only contracts and one migration, but its real MariaDB, FXServer/OneSync, client slice, DoorSystem, IPL/interior, transition, bucket and restart acceptance remains open. The current Core provider-registry additions and the optional `synex_control` operations surface are later Development / Experimental Alpha work; their exact FXServer provider-lifecycle and real CEF client acceptance remains open. `synex_bridge` is an implemented Experimental Alpha Compatibility Platform with separate QB/QBX/ESX providers and fail-closed configuration, not an accepted runtime; examples and every otherwise unlisted downstream resource/library remain experimental snapshots or scaffolds. None is supported by the frozen Core beta.
- `synex_ui` is implemented but remains Experimental Alpha. Type checks, unit/component tests, deterministic browser scenarios, visual regression, production builds, transport validation, and static closed-state checks cannot certify the embedded runtime. Exact production output still needs real FiveM/CEF loading and callback tests, mouse/keyboard/controller switching, safe-zone and ultra-wide checks, focus restoration across close/restart/reconnect, glass/readability review over representative gameplay, and measured closed/open/large-data performance. Until those gates pass, it is not a production-approved UI platform and does not inherit Core acceptance.
- The compatibility catalog ships with no profiles, enabled consumers, identity/group mappings, or active money policies. It contains only three reviewed, `PARTIAL`, non-sensitive `hunger` metadata mappings—one each for QB, QBX, and ESX—and they do not enable a consumer by themselves. Its six cash/bank aliases do not grant transfer authority, and every reviewed surface is `PARTIAL` or `UNSUPPORTED`. Active group projection needs exact group, grade, optional boss-role, and duty mappings; unmapped active memberships and duplicate primary legacy types fail closed. Historical-name facades carry an explicit manifest marker, while a started real `qb-core`, `qbx_core`, or `es_extended` makes its provider reject compatibility calls with `COMPAT_FRAMEWORK_CONFLICT`; QB and QBX lifecycle projections also conflict with each other. Static/headless restart cleanup is covered, but exact-candidate FXServer provider restart, facade conflict, client callback/event, and deployment acceptance remain open. See the [compatibility boundary](compatibility/README.md).
- Bridge usage evidence is bounded and process-local: at most 512 consumer/operation rows per provider and 1,536 combined Control rows. Counters saturate, first/last values are process-relative timer ticks, new keys beyond the bound are omitted with truncation, and consumer/provider restarts remove provider evidence. Core keeps only the newest 512 samples in each Bridge duration histogram before deriving `p50`/`p95`/`p99`. Consumer stop removes both provider warning/usage keys and the coordinator's resolver series/warning keys for that consumer epoch. None of these signals is a durable audit trail, a completion proof, or FXServer/client acceptance evidence.
- Bridge money RPCs carry one trace ID and one UUID operation/idempotency/reference ID through each admitted native transfer and its at-most-one immediate retry after a retryable failure. That bounded retry reuses the exact request and UUID; a separate repeated legacy call receives a new UUID and is not cross-call deduplicated. The checked-in empty profiles, consumers, and money policies still deny all money mutations.
- Control's trace and slow-operation histories are bounded, process-local and restart-empty: at most 512 completed Core spans and 128 slow database-operation aggregates. Its separately projected Core deadlock-occurrence and performed-retry counters are also process-local and restart-empty; an unobserved series remains unavailable rather than being presented as zero. They are not durable or distributed telemetry, do not expose arguments, payloads, raw SQL or parameters, and cannot provide complete cross-domain causality. Raw per-call RPC/hook history and durable latency series remain unavailable. Public oxmysql exposes neither a supported pool snapshot nor Core-observable hard query-timeout events, so both surfaces are explicitly unavailable. The Core incident view lacks cross-provider correlation; Control's self-provider groups only process-local provider health/timeout and resource-transition events in a 60-second temporal window and never asserts root cause. Opaque pagination handles are process-local, player/scope-bound, expire after 120 seconds, and are not durable bookmarks.
- `doctor --bundle --runtime-evidence <file>` accepts an operator-supplied repository-contained JSON file, but it does not collect a live Control/provider snapshot by itself. When no runtime evidence is supplied, the artifact records `UNAVAILABLE` with `RUNTIME_CONTROL_EVIDENCE_NOT_SUPPLIED` instead of synthesizing runtime state. Its TypeScript sanitizer implements the same protection contract as the Lua Control sanitizer, but it is a separate implementation and does not turn imported data into independently verified FXServer evidence.
- Core Control can merge persisted start, heartbeat and active-lease fields only for its current instance. It does not provide remote-instance drill-down or cross-instance Control RPC.
- Core Security Runtime history is a bounded process-local ring, not a durable incident ledger. It defaults to 512 entries, resets on Core restart, and may overwrite old findings with explicit drop/truncation metadata. It records safe rejection summaries only; request/session/source IDs, traces, payloads, details, and secrets are unavailable. Fuzzing and static analysis remain repository gates rather than Runtime findings.
- Groups exposes one bounded client contract, `synex.groups.self.snapshot`; its other 70 contracts are server-only. The self projection has not yet completed an exact-candidate FiveM client smoke test, so it is not a supported gameplay/UI integration boundary.
- Accounts exposes no client contract and no NUI. Its optional `synex_control` provider is a permission-gated read-only diagnostic projection, not a banking UI; financial gameplay and UI remain future `synex_banking` work.
- Entities exposes no client contract or NUI. Its optional Control provider is read-only and cannot spawn, teleport, move, mutate or delete an Entity. Gameplay/client interaction remains a future consuming domain boundary.
- World remains an Experimental Alpha candidate. Its automatic door-relock timers are process-local and are not reconstructed for a persistent door already left unlocked across restart, and the optional visual developer overlay is not included. Its fixed-scale deterministic headless benchmark covers the planned spatial query, Context, Anchor/Door resolution and Access paths, but is not FXServer, OneSync, MariaDB, client, concurrency or capacity evidence. Repository tests and bounded Control/Doctor impact projections do not replace its open MariaDB, FXServer/OneSync, real-client native, transition, bucket and restart acceptance.
- Entity routing buckets and their opaque generations are runtime memory, not durable cross-restart identities. Automatic Entity recovery returns to bucket `0`; a resource must select a current managed bucket explicitly afterward.
- The nearby query uses a bucket-isolated spatial index updated by managed lifecycle/move/checkpoint paths; it is not a continuously authoritative movement feed for arbitrary physics motion. `context.validate` re-reads live player/Entity coordinates before a distance decision.
- `client_observed` Entity state is schema metadata only; there is no client-callable ingestion contract. It is allowed only with `scoped`, the sole replicated mode. FiveM Entity State Bags provide no distinct owner-only path, so Entity State intentionally exposes no `owner` replication mode. The scoped projection still needs live OneSync acceptance.
- Entity resource-owner handoff is not public. `owner.set` changes logical ownership only; cross-resource takeover must not be emulated with direct SQL or native manipulation.
- Public contracts are marked `experimental`; breaking API, schema, and migration changes remain possible before a stable release.
- The current workspace adds Core migration `027_domain_primitives` and the caller-bound DataPort/deletion primitives required by the Organizations Engine. These additions are not covered by the frozen 26-migration Core evidence and need fresh exact-candidate acceptance.
- There is no packaged installer, automatic updater, supported rollback migration, in-place downgrade, or zero-downtime upgrade path. Deployments use reviewed resource copies and forward-only migrations. Backout requires a separately preserved or restored schema compatible with the older Core; older code must never run against a schema already advanced beyond its declared migration inventory.

## Accepted deployment profile

- MariaDB `11.8.8` is the Production-Beta database target. MySQL `8.4` is a documented compatibility target but remains outside the accepted profile until the complete live gate runs against MySQL.
- The initial target topology is one active Core instance using `deny_new`. Multi-instance operation, cross-instance replacement, and `kick_old` remain outside the beta profile until the dedicated two-instance gate passes.
- Exact FXServer artifact, oxmysql version, host operating system, and configuration must be recorded in release evidence. Passing headless tests on another environment does not accept an operator deployment.
- Automated repository tests do not replace a real FiveM client. The accepted Core revision completed its required exact-candidate join, clean disconnect, reconnect, and final cleanup smoke test. Aborted-deferral, repeated-retry, and active-player restart scenarios remain later hardening work unless a release-blocking defect appears.
- A long server soak, permanent evidence runner, historical supported-version upgrade drill, extensive backup/restore certification, and additional non-critical ABI coverage are post-Beta promotion work. Their deferral does not broaden the supported Beta profile or remove normal operator backup responsibilities.

## Runtime and operations

- Planned isolated Core restarts require `synex prepare-restart`. A raw Core restart cannot cancel an interactive oxmysql transaction whose callback is already blocked; use a complete FXServer-process restart for that incident path.
- `database.queryWarnMs` is a slow-operation warning threshold, not a cancellation deadline. The database watchdog closes a health fence but does not cancel the driver operation. Core therefore reports query-timeout telemetry as unavailable instead of relabeling either mechanism as a timeout; operators must monitor database latency and investigate blocked work.
- A DataPort transaction receipt serializes only one exact idempotency key. Different keys may execute concurrently, and receipt capacity is finalized only after the handler response validates; domain handlers still need row locks or compare-and-swap conflict control and cannot safely perform irreversible external effects inside the database transaction.
- A returned runtime database-probe failure or adapter exception closes admission and suspends ordinary database-backed recurring workers. Because that failure has completed, MariaDB restoration can be reconciled automatically after two consecutive successful probes plus dependency and instance-status reconciliation. The fixed five-second watchdog closes the same fence but cannot cancel an oxmysql `Await`.
- oxmysql `2.14.1` can lose the Lua callback when pool `getConnection()` rejects. That timed-out probe never settles, so Core deliberately remains fail-closed and does not launch superseding probes. Restore and verify MariaDB, then perform one controlled restart of the complete FXServer process. A raw Core restart, an oxmysql-only restart, or any Core/FXServer restart loop is unsupported. The connection heartbeat remains active for bounded pending/session authority cleanup, so existing clients are not guaranteed to remain connected during an outage; unrenewable authority fails closed.
- Retention defaults to `retain_forever`. Archive mode mirrors data but does not purge source audit or financial history, so it can increase storage use. Synex does not define an operator's legal retention policy.
- Domain-deletion replay is intentionally finite: all states consume the 10,000-plan global and 1,000-plan per-requester quotas until purge, pending/executing plans are never auto-purged, and terminal plans are eligible for bounded physical purge after 30 days. Once purged, the same idempotency key may create a new plan. Provider schema upgrades also remain unavailable while pending actions require the old version.
- Backup encryption, off-host copies, recovery-point objectives, recovery-time objectives, database privileges, firewalling, secrets, and disaster-recovery ownership remain operator responsibilities.
- The repository provides no high-availability, region-failover, orchestration, hosted control plane, support SLA, or uptime guarantee.

## Security and privacy

- Static security scans and repository certification are guardrails, not proof that a deployment is secure. Host, dependency, ACE, capability, database, and network policy require manual review.
- Logs and acceptance evidence must remain redacted. Never commit Cfx keys, database credentials, raw player identifiers, private endpoints, local test-server state, or database dumps.
- The committed archive/retention behavior is not a legal compliance program. Operators must establish lawful access, retention, erasure, disclosure, and incident-response processes for their jurisdiction.

Use the [release-readiness gate](release-readiness.md) as the canonical decision source and the [backup/restore runbook](backup-and-restore.md) before any Production-Beta deployment.
